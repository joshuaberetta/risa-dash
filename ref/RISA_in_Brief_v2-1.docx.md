# **What is RISA?**

**RISA** is a rapid geospatial assessment methodology that turns field observation and aerial imagery into clear, up-to-date operational maps of displacement sites and other humanitarian locations. It gives responders, coordination mechanisms and decision-makers an accurate visual picture of a site’s physical layout, its infrastructure and its essential services.

Unlike traditional needs assessments, RISA focuses on **what physically exists on the ground**. It combines three complementary sources of information:

* **Field observation —** a standardised KoboToolbox survey records the location, quantity and characteristics of infrastructure through direct observation, not interviews.

* **Aerial imagery —** a high-resolution drone orthomosaic where conditions allow, or recent satellite imagery as an alternative.

* **GIS integration —** the two are combined into a standardised operational map with common symbology.

RISA does not replace assessments such as MIRA or Rapid Needs Assessments. It **complements** them with an infrastructure-focused visual layer that supports operational planning and coordination.

# **Why RISA?**

Humanitarian responses routinely begin without reliable, up-to-date spatial information. Displacement sites appear within days and evolve rapidly as services are established. Sectoral assessments capture needs and vulnerabilities, but rarely show how a site is physically organised. RISA fills that gap with a rapid, standardised map that supports both immediate response and longer-term site management.

**It is not a one-off exercise.** RISA follows a site as it changes — typically an initial assessment 3–5 days after the site is established, a follow-up about a week later, and further updates as the response evolves.

# **How it works**

Every deployment follows the same six-step workflow, which keeps products consistent from one operation and one team to the next:

1. **Mission preparation —** define objectives and sites with partners and authorities; prepare the Kobo survey, imagery, team, equipment, logistics and clearances.

2. **Field data collection —** georeferenced observations via KoboToolbox, and drone flights (or satellite imagery) for site coverage.

3. **Data processing —** validate and clean Kobo data; process imagery into a georeferenced orthomosaic.

4. **GIS integration —** overlay observations on the imagery with standardised symbology and a common map layout.

5. **Dissemination —** share products digitally and in print (A4 / A3 / A0) through coordination channels as fast as possible.

6. **Updating —** repeat as the site evolves to keep a current Common Operational Picture.

# **What RISA produces**

The primary output is a **standardised operational map** that provides a **Common Operational Picture (COP)** — a single reference understood across sectors and organisations. It shows site boundaries, shelters, water and sanitation, health and protection services, logistics areas and access routes, alongside an infrastructure summary table and, where available, a population overview (total, men, women, children).

Alongside the map, RISA can generate a georeferenced infrastructure inventory, a short situation brief for decision-makers, and ready-to-use material for OSOCC briefings, coordination meetings and advocacy.

# **Operational value**

RISA supports decision-making across the response cycle. Its strongest use cases:

* **Initial response planning —** where people are, what services already exist, where the gaps are, and how teams can safely access each area.

* **Coordination & COP —** one shared reference map so authorities, UN agencies, clusters and camp management discuss priorities from the same picture.

* **Service-gap identification —** combining infrastructure and demographic data to reveal underserved areas and target resources.

* **Advocacy & resource mobilisation —** objective visual evidence of shortages and unequal coverage to support discussions with authorities and donors.

* **Remote decision support —** an accurate visual of field conditions for coordinators and headquarters who cannot visit the site.

# **Where RISA has been used**

RISA has been deployed in **Mozambique** (SADC SIMEX) and in the **Venezuela** earthquake response, drawing on the complementary expertise of OCHA Canada (assessment coordination), Kobo specialists (survey), Atlas Logistique (UAV operations and orthomosaic production) and MapAction (GIS and map production). In Venezuela, the drone orthomosaic proved decisive — it revealed handwashing stations, latrines, child-friendly spaces and health services that satellite imagery could not reliably detect.

# **Key messages**

* A rapid, standardised, scalable methodology that turns field observation and aerial imagery into actionable operational products.

* Delivers a Common Operational Picture that supports coordination, planning and evidence-based decisions.

* A complementary geospatial layer to traditional assessments, focused on what physically exists on the ground.

* Flexible by design — drone, satellite or hybrid — adaptable to security, airspace and resource constraints.

* A dynamic monitoring tool that tracks how infrastructure and services change through a response.

* Replicable and transferable — built to be run by any partner or national authority using standard procedures, and extendable beyond displacement sites to EMT locations, logistics hubs, critical infrastructure and debris-management sites.
