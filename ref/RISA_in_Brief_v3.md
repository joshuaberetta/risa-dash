# **What is RISA?**

**RISA** is a rapid way to map what exists on the ground in a displacement site, and whether it works. Field teams record each water point, latrine, health post, distribution point and blocked road, with its status and location. Drone or satellite imagery shows the site's layout. The two are combined into one standard map that everyone in the response can use.

RISA is a **joint initiative of Kobo, MapAction and Atlas Logistique**, endorsed by UNDAC/OCHA (clearance pending). It is not a new organisation. Each partner brings one part: data collection (Kobo), GIS and maps (MapAction), and drone imagery (Atlas).

RISA does not replace MIRA or other needs assessments, and does not interview people. It adds the physical picture: what is there, and what is working.

# **Why RISA?**

In the first days of a sudden-onset disaster, sites appear and change fast, and nobody has a reliable map of them. Sector assessments capture needs, but rarely show how a site is laid out or which services work. UNDAC teams often reach sites before other agencies. A clear picture of conditions on day four has led to latrines, tents and medical teams arriving where they were needed.

# **How it works**

1. **Prepare**: agree sites and objectives with the national authority and partners; adapt the standard form; arrange imagery, authorisations and the data-sharing agreement.

2. **Observe**: field teams record each object, its status (Working, Partly working, Not working, Can't see) and a photo, on phones or tablets that work offline. Drones fly where allowed; recent satellite imagery is the fallback.

3. **Check**: the data is reviewed every evening.

4. **Map**: observations go on the imagery with standard symbols and colours.

5. **Share**: printed and digital maps go out through coordination channels the same day where possible.

6. **Update and hand over**: the map follows the site as it changes, then passes to the organisation that takes over.

# **What you get**

* **A printed map** of the site (A4, A3 or A0) showing services by type, coloured by status, with counts of tents, latrines and water points. It works without internet.

* **A QR code on the map** that opens the online view: the same data, kept up to date and filterable by sector. The online view is the reference; the print is a dated snapshot.

* **A key-findings page** with the map: access, logistics, the services table and the priority gaps, with who should act.

* **The data itself**, in open formats, owned by the national authority as agreed.

* **A feedback QR code** on every map (feedback form TBC, end of 2026).

# **Timeline**

* **First map**: 3–5 days after the site is established.

* **Follow-up**: about a week later.

* **Further updates**: as the response evolves.

* **Handover**: around week 3, to the cluster or sector lead (such as IOM or CCCM) or to the national authority, with mentoring before the team leaves and remote support after.

# **What we ask of the host authority**

* A focal point who works with the team from the start and takes over the maps and data.

* Agreement on priority sites, and support with site access and drone flight authorisations.

* A data-sharing agreement: who owns the data, who may see it, where it is stored and for how long.

* The organisation that will take over at handover, named early.

* Help with local logistics where possible (to agree per deployment, TBC).

# **The toolbox**

The toolbox is built on open standards and open-source tools, so a national team can run RISA itself:

* **This brief**, for decision makers.

* **The RISA SOP**: one procedure, independent of any software, explaining each step and how it commonly fails.

* **Annexes** for technicians: the field form, data collection, drone checklists, GIS standards and map layouts.

* **The standard form** (XLSForm), which runs on KoboToolbox, ODK or Ona.

* **The RISA dashboard**, an open-source tool for the online view and printed maps, which can run on a national server or offline.

Support after handover is through mentoring, webinars, short videos and a contact form, on a best-effort basis. There is no helpdesk with promised response times.

# **Where RISA has been used**

* **Mozambique**, in the SADC simulation exercise (SIMEX).

* **Venezuela**, in the earthquake response. The drone imagery revealed handwashing stations, latrines, child-friendly spaces and health services that satellite imagery could not reliably show.

# **Key messages**

* RISA shows what physically exists in a site and whether it works, within days.

* One standard map gives authorities, UN agencies, clusters and camp managers the same picture.

* A printed map for the field, an online view for the latest data, and a QR code linking the two.

* Open, low-tech and independent of any software, so national teams can run it themselves.

* Drone, satellite or both, depending on what the context allows.

* Built for handover: the data and tools stay with the national authority.

* The pilot focuses on displacement camps in the first weeks; wider uses come later.
