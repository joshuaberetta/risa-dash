## **Summary: what the workshop decided**

Over two days (29 and 30 Oct 2026\) Kobo, MapAction and Atlas agreed to run RISA as a light, non-binding joint initiative and to finish a two-layer toolbox for a camp-focused pilot to show in Durban (23–27 Nov). The "dream platform" (HDP), large-scale funding and a wider scope were pushed to later phases. The group's own framing is that RISA fills the gap in the first days of a sudden-onset response, until a cluster or sector lead takes over around week three.

## **Open questions and risks**

The largest unresolved items are who owns the project manager role, how UNDAC branding is cleared, and what the running cost really is.

### **Unresolved or disputed**

* **Project manager.** The boards show a part-time PM at €50,000 a year. In discussion some argued there is not yet enough work for a full-time job, and suggested a donor paying a share of an existing staff member's salary, with that work backfilled. Kobo is exploring a part-time roster-style role of about 15–20 hours a week. Nobody was named.  
* **Running cost.** The flipchart shows maintenance at €10k a year and a PM at €50k. In the transcript a long-term all-in figure of roughly €100–150k a year was floated and called rough. Confirm which holds before the concept note.  
* **Training budget.** The medium-term training line reads €40,000 on the flipchart, and the transcript does not settle it.  
* **UNDAC / OCHA branding.** UNDAC linkage helps access and credibility, but OCHA cannot simply put its logo on a wider partnership. The idea of an endorsement slot (top left) and an implementer slot (bottom right) was floated, with no decision on how to clear it. HI also needs internal approval for any logo change.  
* **Product, project or system.** The group used the words differently. The working split is a product (the methodology and toolbox) and a project (dissemination, training, feedback); one participant called the end state a system.  
* **Who is on the core team.** Whether to bring other partners (HOT, Flying Labs and others) into the core, or keep them as an advisory or consulting layer, was not settled.  
* **Static map content.** Replace the two inset maps with counts and a legend, or keep both an overview map and the detailed data. Leaning toward keeping an overview and adding the data.  
* **Scope creep.** UNEP / environmental emergencies, community-reported data and preparedness uses were all raised and parked on purpose.

### **Risks**

* **MapAction GIS continuity.** The MapAction colleague drafting the GIS part of the SOP is not able to continue on the project from next month because of extended fieldwork, so that section needs a new owner.  
* **Short runway to Durban.** Durban is about three weeks away, with the SOP due on 14 Nov and a review week before it.  
* **Informal capacity.** Everyone is working from existing workloads; the group agreed each organisation should check its own capacity and risk appetite before committing more.  
* **Handover has no guaranteed owner.** Atlas has little drone capacity in the countries it works in, so in-country follow-up relies on MapAction volunteers, flying labs or the receiving authority.  
* **Aircraft cameras.** Mounting GoPros on helicopters and planes depends on case-by-case pilot permission; the approach is untested outside paper.  
* **Imagery volume and bandwidth.** Many aircraft with two cameras each produce more data than can be uploaded, hence the catalogue-first idea.

### **Parked on purpose**

These went to the parking lot or to later phases: environmental emergencies and infrastructure types beyond camps, helpdesk, how to combine satellite, aerial and drone sources, and funding for joint proposals.

## **What the transcripts add, by theme**

Day 1 wandered across topics and Day 2 turned them into a plan; the points below are regrouped by theme and complement sections 1 to 10, which hold the board content.

### **Toolbox and SOP**

* Two drafts exist: "RISA in brief" (2–3 pages, an AI-assisted draft with a placeholder per organisation) and a fuller SOP, mostly Atlas-written, reusing aerial-assessment network content.  
* Feedback on the SOP: keep the main text non-technical and explain why the workflow is built that way and how it most commonly fails; move field-level detail to annexes.  
* Planned annexes: a printable drone checklist, auto-mosaic workflows per software, a QGIS template where layers are re-pointed to new sources (with a fixed folder structure), and an XLSForm template. Any platform should work, so no click-by-click software guides.  
* Durban audience: NDMA representatives from about six countries, mostly deputy directors and decision makers with some GIS managers. The pitch there is operational value, with technical webinars as a January follow-up.  
* Languages: an English reference document with AI translation and native-speaker review, as Kobo already does for its documentation.

### **Imagery and maps**

* Three image sources: drones (about 5 cm), cameras on helicopters and planes (about 10 cm, an untested idea needing georeferencing), and satellites (about 30 cm), the latter as a fallback where access is constrained.  
* Aircraft cameras: fix two GoPros (nadir and oblique) with the pilot's agreement, then build a metadata-based catalogue in QGIS so analysts request only the areas they need.  
* Map content: standard north arrow, scale, legend and colours per feature type; comparing two dates (30 June and 7 July in the example) shows camp growth and supports site planning. Counts of tents, latrines and other features should sit on the map.  
* Sector detail: a structured form per feature type (WASH, health and so on) would let each agency filter what it needs. Polygons are still drawn by hand, and AI help would still need ground truth.  
* Why it matters: UNDAC often reaches sites before other agencies, and a clear picture of day-four conditions has led to latrines, tents and medical teams arriving.

### **Dream platform (HDP)**

* One platform combining drone imagery, field data and mapping, usable offline, hostable locally, and handed to NDMAs; later used for preparedness and anticipatory work.  
* It should connect to existing tools such as LogIE and the Virtual OSOCC rather than duplicate them.  
* A prototype showing a static and a dynamic view was demonstrated. HI could donate hosting in kind, and Luxembourg is the natural funder.  
* Licensing steered the group to open source; one partner suggested keeping a single ArcGIS licence for interoperability tests.

### **Handover and sustainability**

* UNDAC missions run about three weeks; camps are usually temporary (around 30 days) and are then handed to a sector lead such as IOM or CCCM.  
* Lesson from Madagascar: a Power BI dashboard was left running on the deploying team's credentials, which became awkward to unwind.  
* Support layers discussed: guidebook, in-country mentoring (staying a few extra days to produce the first maps together), remote support, webinars, short videos, and later a chatbot that answers from the toolbox.  
* Atlas has little drone capacity in the countries where it works; MapAction has volunteers and flying labs could help.

### **Training and dissemination**

* Add RISA to UNDAC simulation exercises (the Nairobi induction first), then make it standard; pursue training-of-trainers at regional level through the CONNECT proposal and a three-hour webinar with one hour per organisation.  
* The UNDAC advisory board day at HNPW (about 90 member states) is a chance to present and to approach donors such as Norway.  
* Bahamas project (Oct 2026 to Apr 2027): building information-management skills in the disaster management authority (3–5 staff, monthly sessions), with a wider audience now. It is a pilot site to validate the SOP.

### **Governance and funding**

* An earlier consortium (written "HTA" in the transcript, probably the HDA) is the cautionary tale: a binding MOU caused infighting. Keep documents short, non-binding and flexible.  
* Funding sources named: Luxembourg (interested in joint initiatives), the ECHO call, private-sector donors (Cisco, Google, Microsoft, AWS), UN innovation funds, and an HI foundation campaign. HI's ethics institute must review private-sector partners.  
* UN endorsement strengthens credibility with donors. Each partner applies separately but coordinates, because a joint application needs slow legal paperwork.  
* Hardware estimates: about €2–3k for a light field kit and €6–8k for a GPU kit capable of imagery processing.

### **Signal and the CONNECT proposal**

* Signal was an HI and Atlas project funded by USAID from 2019 to 2025, using secondary data to rate logistics access and vulnerability before an event. It was hard to operationalise and survives only in Madagascar.  
* The April 2026 CONNECT proposal bundled RISA, aerial assessment, Signal and damage assessment as "global commons".
